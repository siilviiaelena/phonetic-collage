English Sounds in Context

1. Consonant Phonemes

Individual consonant sounds that can be difficult for Spanish speakers.

🗣️
think
/θɪŋk/
/θ/ is an unvoiced dental fricative. The tongue is gently placed between the teeth.
👉
this
/ðɪs/
/ð/ is a voiced dental fricative. The tongue is between the teeth and the vocal cords vibrate.
🚢
ship
/ʃɪp/
/ʃ/ is the sound heard in the beginning of “ship”.
🪑
chair
/tʃeər/
/tʃ/ is an affricate sound made with a short stop followed by friction.
🧃
juice
/dʒuːs/
/dʒ/ is a voiced affricate sound.

2. Consonant Clusters

Two or more consonants pronounced together without adding an extra vowel.

🛣️
street
/striːt/
The cluster /str/ contains three consonants pronounced together.
🎮
play
/pleɪ/
The cluster /pl/ begins the word and combines two consonant sounds.
3️⃣
three
/θriː/
The cluster /θr/ combines the /θ/ sound with /r/.
⬛
black
/blæk/
The word begins with the consonant cluster /bl/.
🏫
school
/skuːl/
The word starts with the consonant cluster /sk/.

3. -ED Endings

The ending -ed has three common pronunciations: /t/, /d/, and /ɪd/.

▶️
played
/pleɪd/
The -ed ending is pronounced /d/ because the final sound of “play” is voiced.
🧼
washed
/wɒʃt/
The -ed ending is pronounced /t/ after a voiceless sound.
⏰
wanted
/ˈwɒntɪd/
The -ed ending is pronounced /ɪd/ after the sounds /t/ or /d/.

4. -S Endings

The final -s can be pronounced /s/, /z/, or /ɪz/.

🐱🐱
cats
/kæts/
The final -s is pronounced /s/ after a voiceless consonant.
🐶🐶
dogs
/dɒɡz/
The final -s is pronounced /z/ after a voiced sound.
🚌🚌
buses
/ˈbʌsɪz/
The final -es is pronounced /ɪz/ after sounds such as /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, and /tʃ/.

Common Difficulties for Spanish Speakers

1. /θ/ and /ð/ Spanish speakers may replace these sounds with /t/, /d/, or /s/. A possible solution is to place the tongue gently between the teeth and practice slowly.
2. Consonant Clusters Learners may add an extra vowel between consonants. For example, they may pronounce “street” with an additional vowel. Practice the consonants together without inserting another sound.
3. -ED and -S Endings The spelling does not always correspond to one pronunciation. Practice the three main pronunciation patterns and listen to examples.
